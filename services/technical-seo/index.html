What a technical SEO audit covers

I start by crawling the site with Screaming Frog and reading the site's own Google Search Console data. Together they show what Google can see, what it skips, and where traffic is being lost.

  • Crawling and indexing. Pages that are blocked, orphaned, or wrongly marked noindex, and pages that are indexed but shouldn't be.
  • Sitemaps and robots.txt. A clean XML sitemap that lists only the pages you want in Google, and a robots.txt file that doesn't block them.
  • Redirects and broken links. Redirect chains, 404 errors, and old URLs that still collect links.
  • Speed and Core Web Vitals. Slow pages and slow server responses that hurt both rankings and sales.
  • Duplicate content and canonicals. Filtered and sorted URLs, and near-duplicate pages that split ranking signals.
  • Schema markup. Structured data for products, organizations, and local businesses, so search results can show richer information.

How the work is prioritized

An audit can list hundreds of issues. I rank every fix by its likely effect on clicks and revenue, so the first weeks go to the changes that move the numbers, not to cosmetic warnings.

For KAZA Stores, better crawl efficiency and server performance, together with rewritten titles, helped grow clicks by 54 percent in three months.
